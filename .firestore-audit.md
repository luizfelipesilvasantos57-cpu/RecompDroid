# Firestore data and access audit (prototype)

## Collections and schema

- `users/{uid}`: owner-only profile document. Required fields: `displayName` (string, 1–60), `username` (string, 3–24 ASCII letters/digits/dot/underscore), `updatedAt` (server timestamp). Optional `photoBase64` (base64 JPEG string, at most 700,000 characters). The app reads the exact UID document and writes it with merge semantics.
- `users/{uid}/games/{slug}`: owner-only aggregate per game. Required fields: `title` (string, 1–160), `playSeconds` (integer, 0–315,360,000), `launches` (integer, 0–1,000,000), `lastPlayedAt` (server timestamp). The app lists all documents in this subcollection and merges atomic increments after a tracked game session.

## Access and validation

- Firebase Authentication email/password supplies the UID. Only a signed-in matching UID can read or write their own documents.
- No public reads, cross-account queries, roles, or user deletion are implemented.
- Both create and update validate the final document shape and field types. Extra fields are rejected.
- Account email is not copied into Firestore. Photos are stored within the profile document; the client downsizes them to 256x256 JPEG before upload.
- Current app queries: get `users/{uid}`; list `users/{uid}/games`. No filters, ordering clauses, or composite indexes are used.

## Devil's-advocate review / limitations

- Firestore Rules cannot enforce username uniqueness with this profile-only model. Treat usernames as display labels until a separately designed uniqueness reservation flow is added.
- Client-side duration reporting can be manipulated by a modified client. The cap limits individual stored totals but does not establish trusted anti-cheat playtime.
- The rules are a security prototype. Validate them in the Firebase Emulator and review them before distributing the app.
